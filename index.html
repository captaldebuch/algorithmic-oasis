Research Mission

A New Paradigm for
Arid-Climate Intelligence

The Algorithmic Oasis pioneers Eco-Autonomous Intelligence — a new paradigm where agentic AI systems optimize the complete metabolic cycle of biological life in one of the planet's most demanding environments.

At Sorbonne University Abu Dhabi, we are building a living laboratory where AI agents govern desert flora using water synthesized directly from atmospheric humidity, powered entirely by solar energy. This is not a simulation — it is a functioning, breathing ecosystem steered by intelligence.

The garden's biometric data — soil tension, humidity gradients, water output — feeds a real-time dashboard that makes the invisible visible, bridging environmental science and machine learning in a format accessible to the entire campus community.

Three student research teams — Sensors & Data, Energy & Water, AI & Modelling — collaborate in a format that mirrors professional R&D environments, producing outcomes with direct relevance to the UAE's national sustainability agenda.

Core Systems

Three Technology Pillars

The Algorithmic Oasis is built on three pillars: autonomous water production, intelligent energy management, and a research studio that trains students to work fluently across disciplines — each one essential to the whole.

01
💧

Water Synthesis

Utilizing Ma Hawa AWG technology to extract up to 30 litres of pure water daily from atmospheric humidity — with zero reliance on ground or mains supply. The Predictive Thirst (PT) algorithm determines optimal production windows to minimize energy expenditure during peak heat events.

Readiness Phase 1 Active
02
☀️

Solar Intelligence

A dynamic power management layer using BigBlue solar panels and IP67-rated ELECOM battery systems. An energy AI agent schedules production cycles around irradiance forecasts and dust-storm events, ensuring 24/7 sensor and irrigation autonomy throughout the year.

Readiness Phase 1–2
03
🎓

Interdisciplinary Research Studio

The project is itself a pedagogical architecture. Students from Computer Science, Physics, and the Arts work side by side on a single live system — each bringing a non-redundant perspective. The engineer builds the sensor circuit; the physicist models thermal evapotranspiration; the humanist interrogates what the data means for desert ecology. This cross-domain fluency is the project's most durable output.

Academic Contribution

Research Outputs

Three peer-reviewed research pillars — from AI irrigation systems to interdisciplinary pedagogy — each contributing to the emerging field of sustainable AI in extreme environments.

A

Autonomous Resource Management — The PT Algorithm

Development of the Predictive Thirst (PT) Algorithm: a Reinforcement Learning framework that balances plant biological needs with high-cost water production energy profiles under extreme heat. This work advances the state of the art in agentic AI applied to ecological systems. Target: IEEE Internet of Things Journal or Journal of Cleaner Production.

AI & IoT
B

AWG Performance in Hyper-Arid Coastal Climates

Data-driven performance mapping of the Ma Hawa Mobile Box under variable solar irradiance in Abu Dhabi's hyper-arid coastal climate — generating the first long-term AWG efficiency dataset for this region and contributing to decentralized water production models for the Gulf. Target: Energy and Buildings or Sustainable Cities and Society.

Sustainability
C

Interdisciplinary Research by Design

A methodological contribution: documenting and evaluating the "Oasis Squad" model — a team structure that deliberately integrates students from CS, Physics, and the Arts around a single complex environmental system. The research asks whether cross-domain teams produce more robust AI systems in ambiguous, real-world deployments than single-discipline groups. Target: Journal of Engineering Education or Computers & Education.

Student Impact

Pedagogical Outcomes

The Algorithmic Oasis is a research environment designed as much for the formation of future researchers as for the production of knowledge.

Research Format

The Oasis Squad

Five to six students organized into three paired research teams — Sensors & Data, Energy & Water, AI & Modelling — each responsible for one pillar of the system, collaborating under a shared research agenda.

Academic Output

Master's Thesis Support

At least three Master's theses directly supported across Applied AI, Environmental Physics, and Human-Computer Interaction — with the project integrated into the SUAD Computer Science curriculum as a multi-year undergraduate capstone.

Extreme Deployment

Real-World IoT Engineering

Students gain hands-on experience deploying hardware in conditions of 50°C summer peaks, high coastal humidity, and dust-storm interference — competencies that map directly to regional industry demand for resilient IoT engineers.

Project Timeline

Two-Year Roadmap

A structured progression from indoor proof-of-concept to a fully autonomous outdoor garden — culminating in public installation, academic publication, and an open-source software release.

Year One

Foundation & Digital Metabolism

September – December 2026

Phase 1 — Lab Setup & Sensory Backbone

  • Selection of 5–6 member Oasis Squad
  • Full testing of Ma Hawa GENNY indoor unit
  • APKLVSR soil sensor calibration (per species)
  • ESP32 microcontroller integration
  • MQTT / Cloud real-time monitoring pipeline

January – May 2027

Phase 2 — The Sonified Biosphere

  • Real-time data dashboard (web-based, open to campus)
  • First fully autonomous indoor prototype (V1)
  • Open-source Python AWG optimization library
  • Year 1 interim report to SAFIR Lab Committee

Year Two

Resilience & The Autonomous Chorus

September – December 2027

Phase 3 — Outdoor Hardening & Energy AI

  • Ma Hawa Mobile Box + solar array deployment
  • Reinforcement Learning agent training
  • Stress testing at 50°C / high humidity peak
  • 4G/LTE IoT gateway for remote monitoring

January – June 2028

Phase 4 — The Resonant Garden

  • Public campus web/mobile dashboard launch
  • Public research symposium & campus open day
  • Research paper submissions (3 journals)
  • Master's thesis defences & open-source release
