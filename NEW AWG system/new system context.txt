The key physical principle of **Ma Hawa** technology is **heat exchange-driven condensation** (specifically, cooling air below its dew point to release water vapor as condensate).

### How it works:
| Step | Process |
|------|---------|
| 1 | Air is filtered through multiple stages (nano-ceramic, carbon, metals) to remove pollutants and bacteria  [enterpriseam](https://enterpriseam.com/climate/2023/11/06/uaes-ma-hawa-produces-clean-water-from-air-using-heat-exchange-tech/) |
| 2 | The purified air is **cooled via heat exchange technology**, causing water vapor to condense into liquid water  [enterpriseam](https://enterpriseam.com/climate/2023/11/06/uaes-ma-hawa-produces-clean-water-from-air-using-heat-exchange-tech/) |
| 3 | The resulting **condensate** undergoes three-layer filtration purification  [enterpriseam](https://enterpriseam.com/climate/2023/11/06/uaes-ma-hawa-produces-clean-water-from-air-using-heat-exchange-tech/) |
| 4 | Final UV treatment kills bacteria, and minerals (magnesium, calcium) are added  [enterpriseam](https://enterpriseam.com/climate/2023/11/06/uaes-ma-hawa-produces-clean-water-from-air-using-heat-exchange-tech/) |

The technology leverages **Watergen's patented heat exchange system**, which transforms surrounding air into potable water while conserving energy. It works best in warmer climates with specific humidity levels, like the UAE. The machines consume just **1 kW of electricity to produce 5 liters of water**. Systems 

Three that map well to a teaching lab — increasing complexity, covering thermoelectrics, sorption chemistry, and biomimetic surface physics:

**1. Peltier-based AWG (thermoelectric condenser)**
The cleanest "build it in an afternoon" project — no moving parts, directly demonstrates dew-point cooling. Pair it with a humidity/temperature log so students compute yield vs. RH.
- TEC1-12706 Peltier module(s)
- 12V DC bench supply (5–6 A)
- CPU heatsink + fan for the hot side; smaller heatsink/cold plate for the cold side
- Thermal paste
- DHT22 or SHT31 temp/RH sensor + Arduino/microcontroller for logging
- Condensate collection tray + graduated cylinder
- Small enclosure/ducting to direct airflow over the cold plate

**2. Thermal desiccant AWG (silica-gel absorption/desorption)**
Best for the chemistry/materials angle and the most regionally relevant (arid, sun-rich). Run the two-phase cycle: overnight adsorption, daytime solar regeneration.
- Silica gel (bulk, indicating type so colour-change is visible) — or molecular sieve / activated alumina for comparison
- Mesh trays or breathable sachets to hold the desiccant
- Sealed/transparent solar chamber (acrylic box or repurposed glazing) for desorption
- Black absorber surface + small condenser plate
- Mass balance (to weigh water uptake/release gravimetrically)
- Hygrometer/data logger
- Optional small fan for forced convection during adsorption

**3. Hydrophilic/hydrophobic biomimetic surface (Namib beetle) — passive**
Lowest cost, no power, strong link to surface physics and contact-angle theory. Can be combined with a fog generator for indoor reproducibility.
- Aluminium or acrylic plates as substrate
- Hydrophilic patterning agent (e.g., plasma/UV-ozone treatment, or hydrophilic paint/tape)
- Hydrophobic coating (Rain-X, NeverWet, PDMS, or candle-soot/wax) for the alternating regions
- Stencils/masks to define the bump-and-channel geometry
- Ultrasonic humidifier or fog machine (controlled humidity source)
- Goniometer or phone-macro setup to measure contact angles
- Collection trough + micro-balance for droplet yield

A nice cross-cutting exercise: have all three groups report **Wh per litre** (or "no power") and **yield vs. RH**, so the class can rank them on the same axes the source document uses — efficiency, cost, and climate dependence.
